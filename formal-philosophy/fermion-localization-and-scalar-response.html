A Cubic Cancellation and a Fifth-Order Obstruction

Fermion localization, perturbative mass corrections, and scalar response.

Saumya Bhandari · October 2026
Research status. Independent exploratory mathematical study. The fermionic fields are assumed rather than derived; the result is not a unified theory or a peer-reviewed discovery.

Abstract

A two-channel localization operator exhibits an exact cancellation of the cubic correction to its light eigenmass at a particular localization offset. The fifth-order correction survives and is strictly negative. A separately calculated scalar condensate shows why the cancellation does not imply exact physical Higgs-coupling alignment.

1. Operator and zero modes

Define the first-order operator and its self-adjoint extension:

\(\displaystyle \mathcal H_\varepsilon=\begin{pmatrix}0&A_\varepsilon^\dagger\\ A_\varepsilon&0\end{pmatrix},\quad A_\varepsilon=\partial_z+M_\varepsilon(z).\)
\(\displaystyle M_\varepsilon(z)=bI+a\tanh z\,\sigma_3+\varepsilon\operatorname{sech}z\,\sigma_1.\)

The confinement parameter is (a), the relative offset is (b), and the mixing amplitude is ( arepsilon). At zero mixing the normalized modes are proportional to

\(\displaystyle f(z)=Ne^{-bz}\operatorname{sech}^a z,\qquad g(z)=Ne^{bz}\operatorname{sech}^a z.\)

The normalization and overlap integrals are expressible through beta functions:

\(\displaystyle J(P,Q)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-Qz}\operatorname{sech}^{P}z\,dz=2^{P-1}B\left(\frac{P-Q}{2},\frac{P+Q}{2}\right).\)

2. Perturbative eigenmass

The light mass branch has the expansion (mu(\varepsilon)=c_1\varepsilon+c_3\varepsilon^3+c_5\varepsilon^5+\cdots). Its leading coefficient is

\(\displaystyle c_1=\frac{J(2a+1,0)}{J(2a,2b)}.\)

When all components of the state are permitted to adjust, the cubic term is the difference between induced mixing and normalization terms:

\(\displaystyle c_3=\int\operatorname{sech}z\,uv\,dz-\frac{c_1}{2}\int(u^2+v^2)\,dz.\)

At (b=1/2), the first-order corrections satisfy (u=-g/(2a)) and (v=-f/(2a)). The two cubic contributions cancel. Continuing to fifth order yields the central conditional result:

\(\displaystyle \boxed{\mu(\varepsilon)=\left(1-\frac1{2a}\right)\varepsilon\left[1-\frac{\psi_1(a+1/2)}{16a^4}\varepsilon^4+O(\varepsilon^6)\right],\quad a>\frac12.}\)

Here (psi_1(x)=\sum_{n\ge0}(x+n)^{-2}>0), so the fifth-order coefficient is negative. The cubic cancellation is not an all-orders symmetry.

3. Scalar response and its limitation

If the scalar simply rescales the mixing amplitude, its normalized response is

\(\displaystyle \kappa_{\mathrm{amp}}=\frac{\varepsilon}{\mu}\frac{d\mu}{d\varepsilon}=1-\frac{\psi_1(a+1/2)}{4a^4}\varepsilon^4+O(\varepsilon^6).\)

But the fluctuation of a physical scalar condensate is generally not the same profile as the condensate itself. To check this, consider the energy

\(\displaystyle E[U]=\int dz\left[\frac12(U')^2+\frac12(1-\delta-2\operatorname{sech}^2z)U^2+\frac14U^4\right].\)

Its stationary profile and radial mode satisfy separate equations:

\(\displaystyle -U''+(1-\delta-2\operatorname{sech}^2z)U+U^3=0.\)
\(\displaystyle [-\partial_z^2+1-\delta-2\operatorname{sech}^2z+3U^2]q=\lambda_hq.\)

The physical coupling receives a profile-shape correction that survives at the cubic-cancellation point. This defeats an attempted claim of exact Higgs-like mass proportionality.

4. Interpretation and limits

The calculation supplies a restricted spectral identity, not electron or quark masses. Fermionic statistics, gauge representations, observed Yukawa couplings, and the Standard Model Higgs are not derived. The result's novelty relative to all prior literature has not been independently established.
