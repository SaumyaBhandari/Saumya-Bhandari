Abstract

A predictive model of language observes a flat token history, but discourse itself contains nested scenes, clauses, quotations, intentions, ontological frames, and referential bindings. This paper develops a theorem-first framework for asking whether making that latent structure explicit can reduce the realization cost of prediction without claiming that it reduces language’s intrinsic predictive information. We define an ontology-conditioned nested predictive state (ONPS), distinguish predictive information from model-class-dependent representation cost, and establish six results. First, any deterministic sufficient lift preserves past–future mutual information exactly. Second, the value of context under logarithmic loss is the conditional mutual information I(Y;C∣U)I(Y;C\mid U) and decomposes layer by layer across a context stack. Third, a typed nesting language has infinite ordinary Hankel rank, although it has a finite visibly-pushdown realization; this is a known automata-theoretic separation restated as a language-modeling construction. Fourth, a causal finite-alphabet canonicalization removes literal name identity while preserving equality and type relations for a bounded live-register discipline. Fifth, under finite structural resources and visible nesting, the canonicalized ONPS admits a finite weighted visibly-pushdown realization and hence finite nested Hankel rank. Sixth, for clipped logarithmic loss, the approximation error caused by finite entity, ontology, depth, and continuous-state budgets is bounded by measurable overflow tails plus projection error. We also prove an exact decomposition separating structural prediction from residual lexical realization. The result is a conditional mathematical theory and a set of falsifiable complexity hypotheses. It is not a proof that unrestricted English has finite rank, that a Transformer separation has been established, or that large language models are unnecessary. A companion Version 2 protocol is designed to test those empirical claims end to end.

Central claim. A causal lift cannot erase intrinsic predictive information. It can, however, change the cost of realizing the same predictive information within a restricted computational model class. The scientific question is whether English exhibits a large enough structured-cost separation to matter in practice.

1. Introduction

The string “He got the cup” is locally ambiguous. After a football final, a reader may favor a trophy interpretation; in the ceramics section of a department store, the same local words favor a drinking vessel. The pronoun he also has no stable interpretation without a discourse entity state. The local observation is unchanged. The predictive state is not.

This motivates a representation-level hypothesis:

Some of the apparent computational difficulty of language prediction may arise because a nested, typed, referential process is observed and modeled as a flat token sequence.

The hypothesis is deliberately narrower than “language is simple.” A sufficient representation cannot remove information that the future actually depends on. What can change is the cost of implementing that sufficient statistic in a chosen model class: the number of states, rank, memory organization, parameters, update operations, and decoder complexity. The distinction is essential. It is also exactly where structured automata provide a clean formal precedent: balanced typed nesting has infinite ordinary Hankel rank and no finite weighted word-automaton realization, yet it has a finite visibly-pushdown realization (Carlyle and Paz 1971; Alur and Madhusudan 2004; Labai and Makowsky 2015).

Discourse theory independently motivates structured context. Grosz and Sidner model attentional state with stack-like focus spaces (Grosz and Sidner 1986); dynamic semantic theories maintain discourse referents across sentences (Kamp and Reyle 1993); frame semantics interprets expressions relative to activated background scenes (Fillmore 1982); and discourse logics allow hierarchical as well as nonlocal attachment (Asher and Lascarides 2003). Predictive-state representations and computational mechanics characterize state by predictions of observables rather than by an inaccessible latent variable (Shalizi and Crutchfield 2001; Boots, Siddiqi, and Gordon 2011; Boots, Gordon, and Gretton 2013). Information bottleneck methods formalize the search for compressed states that retain target-relevant information (Tishby, Pereira, and Bialek 2000; Creutzig, Globerson, and Tishby 2009).

The paper combines these lines into a single object: an ontology-conditioned nested predictive state (ONPS). The contribution is not that stacks, ontologies, causal states, register automata, or Hankel matrices are individually new. The contribution is a precise synthesis, a bounded-resource compilation theorem, an approximation envelope, and a clean division between what is proved, what is inherited from prior theory, and what remains an empirical conjecture.

Contributions.

  1. We distinguish intrinsic predictive information from model-class realization cost and prove that a sufficient causal lift preserves the former exactly.

  2. We formalize context nesting, ontology, and reference in ONPS and derive an exact layerwise information signal.

  3. We restate the known ordinary-versus-nested Hankel separation as an exact model-class separation relevant to language modeling.

  4. We define a causal canonicalization for names under a bounded live-register discipline and prove invariance under type-preserving renaming.

  5. We prove a finite structured realization theorem for the bounded canonicalized model and explicitly exclude the unbounded nominal-pushdown case from the claim.

  6. We derive resource-dependent log-loss bounds and an exact structural-versus-lexical uncertainty decomposition.

2. Predictive state and two notions of complexity

Let Ht=(W1,…,Wt)H_t=(W_1,\ldots,W_t) be an observed history and let FtF_t denote a future target, either the entire future or a finite horizon. A causal representation is a measurable map Xt=Φt(Ht),σ(Xt)⊆σ(Ht).X_t=\Phi_t(H_t), \qquad \sigma(X_t)\subseteq\sigma(H_t).Plain-English explanation 01 ↗ The filtration condition forbids future leakage by definition.

Definition 2.1 (Predictive sufficiency). XtX_t is predictively sufficient for FtF_t if Ft⊥Ht∣Xt,F_t\perp H_t\mid X_t,Plain-English explanation 02 ↗ or equivalently ℙ(Ft∣Ht)=ℙ(Ft∣Xt)\mathbb{P}(F_t\mid H_t)=\mathbb{P}(F_t\mid X_t) almost surely.

Proposition 2.2 (No-free-information identity). For every deterministic causal representation Xt=Φt(Ht)X_t=\Phi_t(H_t), I(Ht;Ft)=I(Xt;Ft)+I(Ht;Ft∣Xt).\mathrm{I}(H_t;F_t)=\mathrm{I}(X_t;F_t)+\mathrm{I}(H_t;F_t\mid X_t).Plain-English explanation 03 ↗ Consequently, I(Xt;Ft)≤I(Ht;Ft)\mathrm{I}(X_t;F_t)\leq \mathrm{I}(H_t;F_t), with equality if and only if XtX_t is predictively sufficient.

Proof. Because XtX_t is a deterministic function of HtH_t, the pair (Ht,Xt)(H_t,X_t) carries the same information about FtF_t as HtH_t. Thus I(Ht;Ft)=I(Ht,Xt;Ft).\mathrm{I}(H_t;F_t)=\mathrm{I}(H_t,X_t;F_t).Plain-English explanation 04 ↗ The mutual-information chain rule gives I(Ht,Xt;Ft)=I(Xt;Ft)+I(Ht;Ft∣Xt).\mathrm{I}(H_t,X_t;F_t)=\mathrm{I}(X_t;F_t)+\mathrm{I}(H_t;F_t\mid X_t).Plain-English explanation 05 ↗ The conditional term is nonnegative and vanishes exactly under conditional independence.▫\square

Proposition 2.2 blocks an overclaim. ONPS does not propose that a coordinate transformation destroys intrinsic past–future information. It proposes that the same information may be easier to realize once nesting, equality, and ontology are explicit.

Definition 2.3 (Model-class realization cost). Let PP be a prediction problem, RP(M)R_P(M) the expected risk of a model MM, RP*R_P^* the Bayes risk, c(M)c(M) a declared cost functional, and ℳ\mathcal{M} a model class. Define Cℳ(P,ε;c)=inf{c(M):M∈ℳ,RP(M)≤RP*+ε}.C_{\mathcal{M}}(P,\varepsilon;c) = \inf\left\{c(M): M\in\mathcal{M},\;R_P(M)\leq R_P^*+\varepsilon\right\}.Plain-English explanation 06 ↗ The cost may include state bits, number of parameters, update time, peak memory, and encoder and decoder cost, provided it is declared before evaluation.

Two representations can preserve the same predictive information yet yield different CℳC_{\mathcal{M}}. The exact separation in Section 5 makes this statement non-metaphorical.

3. Ontology-conditioned nested predictive states

Definition 3.1 (ONPS). An ontology-conditioned nested predictive state at time tt is Xt=(qt,Γt,Rt),X_t=(q_t,\Gamma_t,R_t),Plain-English explanation 07 ↗ where:

  • qtq_t is finite local control or a finite-dimensional predictive coordinate;

  • Γt=(γ1,…,γdt)\Gamma_t=(\gamma_1,\ldots,\gamma_{d_t}) is a stack of typed context frames;

  • RtR_t is a live referent register carrying equality, type, role, salience, and finite status features.

A frame may contain an ontology type, a discourse purpose, finite local features, and pointers to live referent slots. The visible structural action is one of CALL\mathrm{CALL}, RETURN\mathrm{RETURN}, or INTERNAL\mathrm{INTERNAL}.

The stack is a first model, not a claim that all discourse is perfectly nested. Right-frontier graphs, shared entity stores, or typed hypergraphs may be required for crossing discourse relations. The theorem below asks what follows if a well-nested component can be exposed.

3.1 Context stacking and the Ronaldo example

Let U=U= “He got the cup.” Let Y=(A,S)Y=(A,S) contain the pronoun antecedent AA and the sense SS of cup. In a football discourse, a possible stack is World⊳Event⊳Sport⊳Football⊳Match,\mathrm{World}\triangleright\mathrm{Event}\triangleright\mathrm{Sport} \triangleright\mathrm{Football}\triangleright\mathrm{Match},Plain-English explanation 08 ↗ whereas a retail discourse may induce World⊳Commerce⊳Shopping⊳DepartmentStore⊳Ceramics.\mathrm{World}\triangleright\mathrm{Commerce}\triangleright\mathrm{Shopping} \triangleright\mathrm{DepartmentStore}\triangleright\mathrm{Ceramics}.Plain-English explanation 09 ↗ A discourse entity register can retain a distribution over candidates for he. The correct formal output is not a forced label but ℙ(A,S∣U,Xt).\mathbb{P}(A,S\mid U,X_t).Plain-English explanation 10 ↗ Context changes posterior odds; it does not logically eliminate all unusual interpretations.

4. The information-theoretic core

Theorem 4.1 (Value of context under logarithmic loss). Let UU be a local utterance, CC an available context, and YY an interpretation variable. Under logarithmic loss, the reduction in Bayes-optimal expected risk obtained by observing CC is RU*−RU,C*=I(Y;C∣U)≥0.R_U^*-R_{U,C}^*=\mathrm{I}(Y;C\mid U)\geq0.Plain-English explanation 11 ↗

Proof. The Bayes-optimal forecast given UU is the true conditional distribution ℙ(Y∣U)\mathbb{P}(Y\mid U), with risk H(Y∣U)\mathrm{H}(Y\mid U). Given (U,C)(U,C), the optimum is ℙ(Y∣U,C)\mathbb{P}(Y\mid U,C), with risk H(Y∣U,C)\mathrm{H}(Y\mid U,C). Their difference is the definition of conditional mutual information.▫\square

The theorem is an information identity, not evidence by itself that any proposed context encoder is compact or cheap. It tells us what a successful representation must retain.

Corollary 4.2 (Layerwise context decomposition). For a context stack C=(F1,…,Fd)C=(F_1,\ldots,F_d), I(Y;C∣U)=∑j=1dI(Y;Fj∣U,F<j).\mathrm{I}(Y;C\mid U) = \sum_{j=1}^{d}\mathrm{I}(Y;F_j\mid U,F_{<j}).Plain-English explanation 12 ↗ For any interpretation value yy with nonzero conditional probabilities, define gj(y)=logℙ(y∣U,F≤j)ℙ(y∣U,F<j).g_j(y)= \log \frac{\mathbb{P}(y\mid U,F_{\leq j})} {\mathbb{P}(y\mid U,F_{<j})}.Plain-English explanation 13 ↗ Then ∑j=1dgj(y)=logℙ(y∣U,C)ℙ(y∣U).\sum_{j=1}^{d}g_j(y) = \log \frac{\mathbb{P}(y\mid U,C)}{\mathbb{P}(y\mid U)}.Plain-English explanation 14 ↗

Proof. The first equality is the mutual-information chain rule. The second follows by multiplying the successive probability ratios and cancelling intermediate terms, then taking logarithms.▫\square

The sequence (g1(y),…,gd(y))(g_1(y),\ldots,g_d(y)) is an exact context-layer information signal. Individual terms may be negative for a particular yy even though their expectation is nonnegative.

Proposition 4.3 (Compression criterion). For any compressed context Z=ϕ(C)Z=\phi(C), I(Y;Z∣U)≤I(Y;C∣U).\mathrm{I}(Y;Z\mid U)\leq\mathrm{I}(Y;C\mid U).Plain-English explanation 15 ↗ Equality holds if and only if Y⊥C∣(U,Z)Y\perp C\mid(U,Z).

This is the correct criterion for a “sentence signal”: not smoothness, visual elegance, or low dimension alone, but sufficiency for the future and interpretation variables of interest.

5. Exact representation separation from nesting

Let m≥2m\geq2 and let c1,…,cmc_1,\ldots,c_m be call symbols with matching returns r1,…,rmr_1,\ldots,r_m. Let DmD_m be the language of properly nested, type-matched words, and let f=𝟏Dmf=\mathbf 1_{D_m}. Its ordinary Hankel matrix is Hf(u,v)=f(uv),u,v∈Σ*.H_f(u,v)=f(uv), \qquad u,v\in\Sigma^*.Plain-English explanation 16 ↗

Theorem 5.1 (Known flat-rank phenomenon, explicit construction). For every k≥1k\geq1, HfH_f contains an mk×mkm^k\times m^k identity submatrix. Hence rank(Hf)≥mkfor every k,\operatorname{rank}(H_f)\geq m^k\quad\text{for every }k,Plain-English explanation 17 ↗ and therefore rank(Hf)=∞\operatorname{rank}(H_f)=\infty.

Proof. For 𝐚=(a1,…,ak)∈[m]k\bm a=(a_1,\ldots,a_k)\in[m]^k, let u𝐚=ca1⋯cak.u_{\bm a}=c_{a_1}\cdots c_{a_k}.Plain-English explanation 18 ↗ For 𝐛=(b1,…,bk)∈[m]k\bm b=(b_1,\ldots,b_k)\in[m]^k, let v𝐛=rbk⋯rb1.v_{\bm b}=r_{b_k}\cdots r_{b_1}.Plain-English explanation 19 ↗ Then u𝐚v𝐛∈Dmu_{\bm a}v_{\bm b}\in D_m exactly when 𝐚=𝐛\bm a=\bm b. The submatrix indexed by these prefixes and suffixes is ImkI_{m^k}.▫\square

This is a direct instance of the standard observation that balanced languages can have infinite ordinary Hankel rank despite finite visibly-pushdown realizations (Labai and Makowsky 2015). It is reproduced because the identity-submatrix proof makes the representation dependence transparent.

Corollary 5.2 (Exact model-class separation). For exact realization of ff over ℝ\mathbb{R}, the finite weighted-word-automaton cost is infinite, whereas the visibly-pushdown cost is finite: CWFA(f,0)=∞,CVPA(f,0)<∞,C_{\mathrm{WFA}}(f,0)=\infty, \qquad C_{\mathrm{VPA}}(f,0)<\infty,Plain-English explanation 20 ↗ when cost is finite exactly for finite-state/finite-stack-alphabet machines.

Proof. By the Carlyle–Paz characterization, a real-valued word function has a finite weighted word-automaton realization if and only if its ordinary Hankel matrix has finite rank (Carlyle and Paz 1971). Theorem 5.1 rules this out. A finite visibly-pushdown automaton recognizes DmD_m by pushing the call type and checking it on return (Alur and Madhusudan 2004).▫\square

Labai and Makowsky prove that a real- or complex-valued well-nested word function is recognized by an nn-state weighted visibly-pushdown automaton if and only if its nested Hankel matrix has rank at most n2n^2 (Labai and Makowsky 2015). The result does not say that every finite-rank realization is small in practice, but it supplies an exact structured realization notion.

Lemma 5.3 (Bounded-window versus stack memory). For every suffix length LL and m≥2m\geq2, there is a typed-nesting next-symbol prediction problem on which every predictor measurable only from the last LL observations has accuracy at most 1/m1/m, while a stack storing one unmatched call type has accuracy 11.

Proof. Choose II uniformly from [m][m] and present the history cIxLc_Ix^L, where xx is an internal filler symbol. The last LL observations are identical for all values of II, so no suffix-only predictor can distinguish the correct return rIr_I. Its best accuracy is the largest prior mass, 1/m1/m. A stack stores II when cIc_I arrives and predicts rIr_I exactly.▫\square

Remark 5.4 (No Transformer separation). Lemma 5.3 separates bounded suffix memory from explicit stack memory. It does not prove a separation from attention, recurrent networks, structured state-space models, or Transformers. Neural stack and stack-attention architectures already demonstrate that explicit structural memory can be combined with neural sequence models (DuSell and Chiang 2020; Li et al. 2024). The empirical question is comparative cost, not representability in the abstract.

6. Referential symmetry and causal canonicalization

Literal names come from a large or open-ended alphabet, but many structural predictions depend only on type and equality: whether the present mention is the same entity as a live referent, not whether its surface form is Ronaldo. Register and nominal automata provide established foundations for computation over such infinite alphabets (Kaminski and Francez 1994; Schroeder et al. 2016; Bojanczyk et al. 2021).

Let 𝒯\mathcal{T} be a finite type set. For each τ∈𝒯\tau\in\mathcal{T}, let 𝒩τ\mathcal N_\tau be a countably infinite set of names of type τ\tau, and assume the sets are disjoint. Let 𝒢=∏τ∈𝒯Sym(𝒩τ)\mathcal{G}=\prod_{\tau\in\mathcal{T}}\mathrm{Sym}(\mathcal N_\tau)Plain-English explanation 21 ↗ be the group of type-preserving name permutations.

A BB-register configuration consists of at most BB occupied slots. Each occupied slot carries a name, its type, finite attributes, and a pin bit. A stack frame may contain only finite frame/ontology attributes and pointers to register slots. A referenced slot is pinned until the frame that references it returns; only unpinned slots may be overwritten. Thus BB bounds all names that remain structurally live anywhere in the current state.

Definition 6.1 (Equivariance). A transition kernel TT and structural output map OO are type-equivariant if, for all g∈𝒢g\in\mathcal{G}, T(g⋅x,g⋅a)=g⋅T(x,a),O(g⋅x)=O(x).T(g\cdot x,g\cdot a)=g\cdot T(x,a), \qquad O(g\cdot x)=O(x).Plain-English explanation 22 ↗ Name-valued outputs may be equivariant rather than invariant; the present theorem concerns structural predictions modulo renaming.

6.1 Finite canonical observations

Given a current register state RR and a name token n∈𝒩τn\in\mathcal N_\tau, define the causal code κR(n)={EQ[i],n is equal to the name in occupied slot i,NEWτ,n is unequal to every live register name.\kappa_R(n)= \begin{cases} \mathrm{EQ}_{[i]}, & n\text{ is equal to the name in occupied slot }i,\\ \mathrm{NEW}_\tau, & n\text{ is unequal to every live register name}. \end{cases}Plain-English explanation 23 ↗ Here [i][i] denotes the equality block of slot ii if duplicate slot values are permitted. Non-name observations retain their finite labels. Allocation and eviction actions are included in the canonical transition symbol and are determined from current finite control and the code.

Theorem 6.2 (Causal name-canonicalization). Consider two raw histories related by a type-preserving permutation g∈𝒢g\in\mathcal{G}, initialized from correspondingly permuted register states. Under an equivariant update rule and the same finite allocation discipline, their canonical observation traces are identical, and their states remain in the same 𝒢\mathcal{G}-orbit at every time. Conversely, the canonical trace and initial orbit determine the orbit sequence, though not the literal names.

Proof. Proceed by induction on time. The claim is true initially. Suppose the two current states are related by gg. Equality of the next name to each live register is preserved by a bijection, and type is preserved by definition of 𝒢\mathcal{G}; therefore the next canonical codes agree. The finite allocation action is the same because it is a function of finite control and the canonical code. Equivariance of the update maps the next states into corresponding orbits. The converse follows because the canonical transition rule is deterministic on orbits. Literal names are intentionally discarded.▫\square

The theorem states exact invariance under name permutation. It also states the limitation: once a name is evicted from every live register, its later reappearance is treated as new. Persistent identity across unbounded inactive history is outside this bounded semantics.

6.2 Finite orbit count

Let AA be the number of finite attribute combinations per occupied slot. The number of canonical register orbits is finite. A convenient crude upper bound is NB,𝒯,A≤4BBell(B)(|𝒯|A)B.N_{B,\mathcal{T},A} \leq 4^B\operatorname{Bell}(B)(|\mathcal{T}|A)^B.Plain-English explanation 24 ↗ The factors overcount occupancy, pin status, equality partitions, types, and attributes; tightness is not needed.

Theorem 6.3 (Bounded ONPS structured realization). Assume:

  1. finite local control QQ and finite discrete predictive coordinate set ZZ;

  2. finite ontology, frame, role, status, and discourse-operation alphabets;

  3. a visible call/return/internal partition after the causal context lift;

  4. the BB-register pinned discipline above, with every stack pointer targeting a live slot;

  5. type-equivariant transitions and structural outputs; and

  6. finite transition/output weights over ℝ\mathbb{R} or ℂ\mathbb C.

After causal name-canonicalization, the structural word function is recognized by a finite weighted visibly-pushdown automaton. It may be chosen with at most n≤|Q||Z|NB,𝒯,An\leq |Q|\,|Z|\,N_{B,\mathcal{T},A}Plain-English explanation 25 ↗ canonical control states and a finite stack alphabet. Consequently, its nested Hankel rank is at most n2n^2.

Proof. By Theorem 6.2, raw name identity can be replaced online by a finite code consisting of equality-to-live-slot or a typed-new symbol. Register configurations modulo 𝒢\mathcal{G} have at most NB,𝒯,AN_{B,\mathcal{T},A} orbits. The product with finite QQ and ZZ is therefore finite. A stack symbol contains only finite frame/ontology/status labels and finitely many pointers in {1,…,B}\{1,\ldots,B\}, so the stack alphabet is finite. The action partition is visible by assumption. The orbit-level weighted transition maps therefore define a finite weighted visibly-pushdown automaton. The nested-Hankel bound follows from Labai and Makowsky’s characterization (Labai and Makowsky 2015).▫\square

Limitation 6.4 (The theorem is conditional). Theorem 6.3 is a sufficiency theorem. It does not establish that English has a finite ontology, bounded live reference, perfectly visible nesting, or a finite useful predictive coordinate. Those are approximation parameters to be measured, not linguistic facts inferred from the theorem.

Limitation 6.5 (Unbounded persistent names). If arbitrarily many distinct names remain recoverable through an unbounded stack, the system is not compiled by the finite classical VPA construction above. It belongs to nominal/register-pushdown territory, where decidability and equivalence behavior can be substantially harder; some combinations of registers and pushdown storage lead to undecidability results (Murawski, Ramsay, and Tzevelekos 2020). A weighted nominal nested-Hankel realization theorem would be a separate theoretical contribution and is left open.

7. Approximation theory for finite resources

Exact finiteness is the wrong empirical question. Let an ideal causal state use arbitrary resources, and let XtB,K,D,RX_t^{B,K,D,R}Plain-English explanation 26 ↗ be an approximation with active-entity budget BB, ontology budget KK, explicit depth budget DD, and continuous predictive dimension RR.

Let ΩtB,K,D\Omega_t^{B,K,D} be the event that at least one discrete resource overflows: more than BB live referents are required, context depth exceeds DD, or the required ontology item is outside the retained KK-item ontology. Let pt*p_t^* be an oracle predictive distribution and qtB,K,D,Rq_t^{B,K,D,R} the bounded predictor. Define clipped log loss ℓα(q,y)=−logmax{q(y),α},0<α<1.\ell_\alpha(q,y)=-\log\max\{q(y),\alpha\}, \qquad 0<\alpha<1.Plain-English explanation 27 ↗ Its range is at most Lα=log(1/α)L_\alpha=\log(1/\alpha).

Theorem 7.1 (Resource-overflow risk envelope). Suppose qtB,K,D,R=pt*q_t^{B,K,D,R}=p_t^* whenever ΩtB,K,D\Omega_t^{B,K,D} does not occur and the continuous state is represented exactly. Then Rα(qB,K,D,R)−Rα(p*)≤Lαℙ(ΩtB,K,D).R_\alpha(q^{B,K,D,R})-R_\alpha(p^*) \leq L_\alpha\mathbb{P}(\Omega_t^{B,K,D}).Plain-English explanation 28 ↗ By the union bound, Rα(q)−Rα(p*)≤Lα[ℙ(Nlive>B)+ℙ(dt>D)+ℙ(ot∉𝒪K)].\begin{aligned} R_\alpha(q)-R_\alpha(p^*) \leq L_\alpha\big[&\mathbb{P}(N_{\mathrm{live}}>B) +\mathbb{P}(d_t>D)\\ &+\mathbb{P}(o_t\notin\mathcal{O}_K)\big]. \end{aligned}Plain-English explanation 29 ↗ If, outside overflow, the RR-dimensional approximation has expected total variation at most ηR\eta_R, then Rα(q)−Rα(p*)≤Lαℙ(ΩtB,K,D)+2ηRα.R_\alpha(q)-R_\alpha(p^*) \leq L_\alpha\mathbb{P}(\Omega_t^{B,K,D})+\frac{2\eta_R}{\alpha}.Plain-English explanation 30 ↗

Proof. When no overflow occurs and the state is exact, the clipped losses agree. On overflow, the excess loss is at most the full range LαL_\alpha. Taking expectations gives the first inequality; the second follows from the union bound. For the approximation term, the function x↦−logmax{x,α}x\mapsto-\log\max\{x,\alpha\} is 1/α1/\alpha-Lipschitz on [0,1][0,1]. Therefore the pointwise loss difference is at most ∥pt*−qt∥1/α=2TV(pt*,qt)/α\|p_t^*-q_t\|_1/\alpha=2\operatorname{TV}(p_t^*,q_t)/\alpha. Take expectations and add the overflow term.▫\square

The theorem converts hidden assumptions into measurable curves. Rather than assert “bounded active referents,” Version 2 estimates ℙ(Nlive>B)\mathbb{P}(N_{\mathrm{live}}>B) for increasing BB. Rather than assert “finite ontology,” it reports residual risk as KK grows. A useful theory predicts favorable tails and diminishing error at modest cost.

8. The lexical-realization barrier

Structural state compression does not automatically yield cheap next-token generation. Let WW be the next token and let S=g(W)S=g(W) be a deterministic structural or semantic coarsening, such as part of speech, ontology class, referent role, or semantic event type.

Proposition 8.1 (Structural and lexical uncertainty decomposition). For every history HH, H(W∣H)=H(S∣H)+H(W∣S,H).\mathrm{H}(W\mid H) = \mathrm{H}(S\mid H)+\mathrm{H}(W\mid S,H).Plain-English explanation 31 ↗

Proof. Because SS is a deterministic function of WW, H(S∣W,H)=0\mathrm{H}(S\mid W,H)=0. The chain rule gives H(W,S∣H)=H(S∣H)+H(W∣S,H)\mathrm{H}(W,S\mid H)=\mathrm{H}(S\mid H)+\mathrm{H}(W\mid S,H)Plain-English explanation 32 ↗ and also H(W,S∣H)=H(W∣H)\mathrm{H}(W,S\mid H)=\mathrm{H}(W\mid H).▫\square

The first term can collapse while the second remains large. Therefore a theorem about nested structural realization is not yet a theorem about low-perplexity lexical generation. Version 2 must report both terms and include encoder and decoder cost in every claim.

9. The formal research hypotheses

The theory separates three logically independent hypotheses.

Conjecture 9.1 (Nested-state hypothesis, H1). A measurable fraction of long-range predictive dependence in English is represented more economically by explicit nested state than by matched flat-history models.

Conjecture 9.2 (Referential-symmetry hypothesis, H2). A measurable fraction of entity-related prediction is invariant under type-preserving renaming and is captured by bounded equality/binding state rather than literal name identity.

Conjecture 9.3 (Complexity-collapse hypothesis, H3). At matched held-out predictive risk, the complete causal ONPS system—encoder, state update, and decoder—achieves a strictly better state/compute Pareto frontier than matched flat neural sequence baselines.

H1 and H2 may hold while H3 fails. Structural prediction may simplify while lexical realization dominates. A favorable rank diagnostic may fail to translate into latency. These outcomes are scientifically distinct.

11. Consequences and non-consequences

The formal results imply:

  • realization complexity is model-class dependent;

  • flattening a nested process can produce infinite ordinary rank despite finite structured realization;

  • context layers have exact, additive information contributions;

  • bounded live name identity can be quotiented by type-preserving symmetry in a causal way;

  • resource assumptions can be converted to measurable approximation errors.

They do not imply:

  • that English has finite nested Hankel rank;

  • that finite rank means small parameter count or cheap inference;

  • that a stack is the complete structure of discourse;

  • that ONPS can be inferred cheaply from raw text;

  • that exact lexical generation is low entropy;

  • that Transformers cannot represent nesting;

  • that current LLMs can be bypassed.

The disruptive claim becomes defensible only if Version 2 finds a leftward shift in the end-to-end predictive-performance-versus-cost frontier, under strict causality, matched baselines, adversarial invariance controls, and an untouched final test set.

12. Discussion

The central conceptual move is to treat language history as an observation of a structured predictive process, not to declare sentences literal mechanical systems. The mathematical precedent is strong: state dimension, rank, and memory can change dramatically when a representation exposes the algebraic composition law that the flat sequence hides. The Dyck construction is an exact example. It proves possibility, not prevalence.

The most important object is not a waveform but a causal quotient. Histories that induce the same future-and-interpretation distribution should be identified. ONPS proposes that a useful approximation to that quotient factors into nested frames, ontology, referential equality, and a small residual coordinate. This is falsifiable: if raw history still provides large predictive gains after conditioning on a cheap ONPS state, the proposed factorization is inadequate.

The bounded theorem is intentionally restrictive. It establishes a clean base class from which richer theories can grow. There are at least three extensions: graph-structured discourse beyond a stack; nominal pushdown state with persistent unbounded names; and continuous predictive coordinates learned from observable moments. Each extension should preserve the discipline used here: define the representation, define its composition law, derive the appropriate realization object, and state what empirical tail or approximation error replaces exact finiteness.

13. Conclusion

A causal representation cannot make genuine predictive information disappear. It can make that information less expensive to realize. Nested-word theory supplies an exact case in which a flat finite-state representation is impossible while a finite structured realization exists. Discourse theory suggests that language contains analogous nested and referential organization. ONPS turns that suggestion into a bounded mathematical object, derives a causal name-invariant compilation, and replaces hidden finiteness assumptions with measurable resource-error curves.

The theoretical claim of Version 1 is therefore precise:

If a linguistic process admits a causal, bounded, ontology-conditioned nested predictive state, then its structural prediction can have finite nested realization complexity even when its flat ordinary realization complexity is unbounded; the approximation error of finite resource budgets is controlled by observable overflow and projection terms.

Whether English occupies that favorable regime, and whether the end-to-end saving survives encoder and lexical-decoder costs, is the subject of Version 2. A positive result would challenge current assumptions about how language prediction should be implemented. A negative result would delimit exactly how far nested contextual structure can simplify the problem.

C. Version 2 preregistration summary

The companion computational version is frozen around the following requirements:

  1. every state is strictly online: Xt=Φ(W≤t)X_t=\Phi(W_{\leq t});

  2. all encoder, state-update, and decoder costs are included;

  3. resource curves vary (B,K,D,R)(B,K,D,R) rather than asserting finiteness;

  4. raw, neural, structured, hybrid, shuffled, and random-feature baselines are capacity matched;

  5. name permutation, context shuffling, lexical substitution, and nesting destruction test invariance;

  6. structural and lexical targets are reported separately;

  7. analysis, held-out, and final-test books are split by primary author;

  8. the final-test split is opened once after code and metrics are frozen.
